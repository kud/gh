A third tier that gives the overflow rows a verdict, and a correction to why the first two exist.

The two-tier fetch buys 100 review requests for one point by dropping the health selection whole, which is honest and which leaves ~80 of them `unknown`. That is the right answer to "we did not look", and it is the wrong place to stop on the one account that most needs the board to say whose move it is. `healthIdsFrom`, `buildHealthQuery`, `buildHealthQueries` and `mergeHealth` close it: the ids of the rows that came back without health, asked for again with the full `PR_HEALTH` and `PR_CONVERSATION` selections, merged back by node id. Measured on a live account with 102 matching review requests, that is 56 points and about six seconds of wall clock, against a 5,000/hour budget and a ten-minute cache, and nothing at all for an account that never truncates.

**The 502 was never the search.** The note on `limits` said it was — "the search timing out at GitHub's proxy" — and the correction matters more than the feature, because that reading makes the obvious fix look free: address the rows by node id and skip the search index entirely. It is not free, because the search index was not the problem. A `nodes(ids:)` lookup carrying the same selections 502s twice at 100 ids, at the same eleven seconds, having touched no search at all. It answers at 8.7s for 50 and 4.0s for 25. What dies is the resolver expanding twelve thousand nodes of check rollups and review threads, whichever door the request came through. So `HEALTH_BATCH_SIZE` is the mechanism rather than a tuning knob: a tier three that fired one 100-id request would reproduce the exact failure it was written to fix.

Both fragments travel together, and health alone would have been a bug wearing the shape of an optimisation. `computeHealth` reads `unresolvedThreads` out of `reviewThreads`, which lives in `PR_CONVERSATION` — so a health-only enrichment reports `waiting` on a PR with open threads, which is the silent wrong token this set is all-or-nothing to prevent, one layer down. It also costs almost nothing to carry: `reviewThreads` is most of what a PR drags, so the conversation rides along and an enriched overflow row comes out fully equal to a first-tier one, last actor and activity age intact.

PR nodes now select `id` in every source and in both shapes. Nothing could be addressed without it, and it is identity rather than health — it belongs beside `number`, where dropping the health selection cannot take it along. Per-source would have been the cheaper edit and the more expensive mistake: the first time another source truncates, the asymmetry costs an hour before anyone thinks to look at the fragment.

`mergeHealth` merges a node only when every health key is present and the rollup is non-null, and it does so per node rather than per response. GraphQL can answer 200 with errors and null fields, and a node carrying `reviewDecision` with a null `statusCheckRollup` is the one shape that defeats the guard downstream — `hasHealthSelection` reads true on the first key while `computeHealth` sees zero checks and returns `waiting`. A partial answer should cost you verdicts, never buy you a wrong one. A batch that fails merges nothing, a node that merges nothing keeps no health keys, and `whoseMove` goes on answering `unknown` — the invariant holds by construction, with no flag for a caller to forget.
